TITLE OF INVENTION

Battery Thermal Management System Using Distributed Temperature Sensors

ABSTRACT

A battery thermal management system includes a plurality of temperature sensors distributed across the cells of a battery pack. A controller receives temperature readings from the sensors, estimates the internal temperature of each cell using a thermal model, and adjusts the flow rate of a liquid coolant through cooling channels to keep every cell within a target temperature range. The system reduces thermal runaway risk and extends battery life.

TECHNICAL FIELD

The present disclosure relates to electric vehicle batteries, and more particularly to monitoring and controlling the temperature of individual lithium-ion cells.

BACKGROUND

Lithium-ion cells degrade quickly when operated above 45 degrees Celsius. Conventional battery packs place a single temperature sensor near the pack outlet, which cannot detect hot spots in individual cells. As a result, a failing cell may overheat before the controller reacts, which can lead to thermal runaway.

SUMMARY

According to one aspect, a battery thermal management system comprises temperature sensors attached to each cell, a coolant pump, and a controller. The controller estimates the core temperature of each cell from its surface temperature and its charging current. When the estimated core temperature of any cell exceeds a threshold, the controller increases the coolant flow rate through the channel adjacent to that cell.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

[0010] As shown in FIG. 1, the battery pack 100 includes twelve cells 102 arranged in two rows. Each cell 102 carries a thermistor 104 bonded to its casing with thermally conductive adhesive.

[0011] The controller 110 samples every thermistor 104 at 10 Hz. A first-order thermal model estimates the core temperature from the measured surface temperature and the cell current. The model parameters are calibrated during manufacturing.

[0012] Cooling channels 120 run between the rows of cells. A variable-speed pump 122 circulates a water-glycol coolant through the channels 120. The controller 110 sets the pump speed using a proportional-integral control law so that the hottest estimated core temperature stays below 40 degrees Celsius.

[0013] If the estimated core temperature of a cell rises faster than 2 degrees Celsius per minute, the controller 110 reports a fault and limits the charging current.

What is claimed is:

A battery thermal management system comprising: a plurality of battery cells; a temperature sensor attached to each battery cell; a coolant pump configured to circulate a liquid coolant through cooling channels adjacent to the battery cells; and a controller configured to estimate a core temperature of each cell and to adjust a speed of the coolant pump based on the estimated core temperatures.

5. A method of cooling a battery pack, comprising: measuring a surface temperature of each cell; estimating a core temperature of each cell; and increasing a coolant flow rate when any estimated core temperature exceeds a threshold.
